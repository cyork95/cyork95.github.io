Nobody cooks from an empty kitchen every night. You stock staples — flour, salt, oil, the spice blend you put on everything — and grab them without thinking. The meal changes; the pantry doesn't.

Your prompts deserve the same setup. Think about how much context you retype from memory, slightly differently, every single time: who you are, what the project is, who the audience is, how you like things formatted, the three rules you always forget to mention until the answer comes back wrong. Retyped context has two failure modes — you leave something out, or you phrase it a little differently and get a slightly different cook. Usually both, on alternating days.

A **context block** is a staple: a short, named, reusable chunk of context you write once, keep where you can grab it, and drop into any prompt that needs it. A block for your role. A block for the project. A block for your house style. Compose a prompt by grabbing blocks off the shelf and adding only what's specific to today's task.

Good blocks share four traits:

- **Self-contained.** A block makes sense pasted into any conversation, with nothing around it.
- **Named.** "Team status block" beats "that paragraph I wrote in March" — you should know what you're grabbing.
- **Current.** A pantry gets restocked; a block describing last quarter's project is a stale ingredient, and stale is worse than missing (that's Lesson 4).
- **Short.** Blocks get spent in every prompt they join. Five good lines beat two rambling paragraphs — context is a budget (that's Lesson 5).

This site keeps a head start for you: **The Pantry** stocks ready-made blocks — role frames, context setters, format shapers — across six shelves. Steal those, then write the ones only you can write: yours.

### Before / after

❌ **Cooking from memory, every Friday**

> Write my weekly status update. I'm on the platform team, we work on data pipelines, my manager likes brief updates with blockers called out, format it as Shipped / In Progress / Blocked, keep it factual… *(typed from memory — last week you forgot the blockers rule; the week before, the format)*

✅ **Grabbing the block**

> **[Block: MY STATUS UPDATE]** I'm a data engineer on the platform team (data pipelines). Audience: my manager, who reads fast. Format: three sections — Shipped / In Progress / Blocked. Factual, no hype, blockers always named with an owner.
>
> This week's raw notes: finished the vendor API migration; review pending on the backfill job; blocked on warehouse credentials, owner is Priya.

The first prompt is a new act of memory every week, and its quality depends on your Friday-afternoon brain. The second is the same block every week, with only the news changing. The output gets consistent — because the input finally is.

### Try it

Write your first block today: five lines maximum — who you are, what you're usually working on, who it's for, and what good output looks like for you. Save it somewhere you'll actually grab it: a notes app, a text expander, a doc called "pantry."

Use it three times this week *without editing it*. Then revise it once, based on what you kept wishing it said. That's the whole method: stock, grab, restock.
