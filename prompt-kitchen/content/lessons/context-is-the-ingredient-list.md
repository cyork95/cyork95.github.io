Walk into a kitchen and say "make dinner," and even a great cook is stuck guessing. They don't know who's eating, what's in the fridge, or whether you hate cilantro. They'll make something — probably the most average dinner imaginable.

An AI model is in exactly that position every time you prompt it. It doesn't know who you are, what you're working on, or what a good result looks like for you. So when your prompt leaves gaps, the model doesn't stop and ask. It cooks anyway — and fills every gap with the most average choice available.

That's the part most people miss: missing context doesn't produce no answer, it produces a generic one. Ask for "a cover letter" with nothing else and you won't get an error. You'll get a competent letter for a person who doesn't exist, applying for a job that isn't yours, in a voice that isn't anyone's. Every fact you left out became a decision the model made for you — averaged from everyone else's letters.

Context is the ingredient list you lay on the counter before cooking starts. Not a longer prompt for its own sake — the specific facts that change the dish:

The facts — dates, names, numbers, the actual situation. The model can't use what it can't see.

The audience — who's this for, and what do they already know?

The constraints — what must be in, what must stay out, what would make the answer useless even if it's well-written?

Lay those out and the same model, with the same skills, suddenly cooks like it knows your kitchen — because now it does.

### Before / after

❌ No ingredients

Write an email to my team saying the deadline moved.

What comes back is polite, professional, and invented: a made-up reason, a guessed tone, maybe a new date it hallucinated to fill the hole. You end up rewriting it — which means you were the context, just delivered the slow way.

✅ Ingredients on the counter

Write a short email to my team of 6 engineers: our launch deadline moves from Nov 14 to Nov 21 because the payment vendor's API isn't ready. Matter-of-fact tone, no blame. Make clear the scope is unchanged, and that Friday's demo moves to the following Tuesday. Under 120 words.

Every clause is an ingredient: who it's for, the real dates, the real reason, the tone, the two facts that must survive, the length. There's almost nothing left to guess — so almost nothing gets invented.

### Try it

Take the last prompt you actually sent an AI this week and re-read it as a stranger. List every fact you assumed it knew — then circle the ones that were only in your head. Add just those, re-run the prompt, and compare the two answers side by side.

That gap between the answers is what context buys you. It was never about prompt magic. It's ingredients.
